# General sumary

### Paper Identification & Metadata
- **Title**: Comparison between epileptic seizure prediction and forecasting based on machine learning
- **Authors**: Gonçalo Costa, César Teixeira, and Mauro F. Pinto
- **Affiliation**: Center for Informatics and Systems of the University of Coimbra (CISUC), Department of Informatics Engineering, University of Coimbra, Portugal
- **Publication Venue**: *Scientific Reports* (Nature Portfolio), Vol. 14, Article 5653, 2024, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-56019-z
- **Paper Type**: `proposal`
- **Task**: `prediction and forecasting` (direct comparison of crisp binary preictal prediction vs. continuous probabilistic seizure forecasting using identical patient-tailored machine learning pipelines)

### Population & Clinical Setting
- **Subject Population**: Adult cohort with Temporal Lobe Epilepsy (TLE), the most common type of focal epilepsy (40 patients: 17 females, 23 males; mean age $41.43 \pm 15.87$ years).
- **Clinical Environment**: Inpatient presurgical monitoring at Universitätsklinikum Freiburg, Germany, characterized by anti-epileptic drug (AED) tapering and sleep deprivation designed to induce seizures.

### Dataset & Volume Overview
- **Dataset**: European Database on Epilepsy (EPILEPSIAE).
- **Inclusion Criteria**: Patients experiencing at least four lead seizures with a minimum spacing of 4.5 hours between consecutive seizures.
- **Data Volume**:
  - Total duration: 5,484 hours of continuous scalp EEG containing 224 seizures.
  - Training Set: First 3 seizures per patient (120 seizures total, 3,889 hours, mean $4.05 \pm 2.58$ days per patient).
  - Testing Set: Remaining seizures per patient (104 seizures total, 1,595 hours, mean $1.66 \pm 1.24$ days per patient).
- **Montage & Sampling**:
  - Scalp EEG sampled at 256 Hz using 19 electrodes arranged according to the International 10–20 System.
- **Exclusions**: Patients with fewer than 4 lead seizures or insufficient inter-seizure spacing (< 4.5 hours) were excluded from the cohort. Supplementary Tables S1–S4 were not accessible within the main text.

# Data sumary

### Data Preprocessing & Segmentation
- **Automated Artifact Removal**:
  - Preprocessed with a trained deep Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) artifact cleaner (Lopes et al.) trained to replicate clinical expert annotations. Automatically suppressed ocular, cardiac, electromyogenic, and electrode connection artifacts without human intervention.
- **Segment Structure**:
  - Non-overlapping 5-second windows (1280 samples at 256 Hz).
- **Preictal Horizon Definition**:
  - Preictal state (level 1) divided into Seizure Prediction Horizon (SPH) fixed at 10 minutes (adequate for rescue medication intake) and Seizure Occurrence Period (SOP) explored across 7 durations: 20 to 50 minutes in 5-minute increments. Interictal state (level 0).
  - Features standardized using z-score normalization calculated on training data.
- **Class Imbalance Strategy**:
  - Ratio of preictal to interictal samples was approximately $10^{-2}$.
  - *Logistic Regression*: Inverse class weighting: $w_i = N_{\text{total}} / (2 \cdot N_i)$.
  - *SVM and SNN Ensembles*: Systematic random undersampling of the interictal class, dividing interictal time into $n$ groups ($n = \text{number of preictal windows}$) and selecting one random window per group to preserve full signal temporality.

### Feature Extraction & Selection
- **Feature Space (59 univariate linear features $\times$ 19 channels = 1,121 features per window)**:
  - *Frequency Domain*: Relative spectral power in Delta (0.5–4 Hz), Theta (4–8 Hz), Alpha (8–13 Hz), Beta (13–30 Hz), and four Gamma sub-bands (30–47 Hz, 53–75 Hz, 75–97 Hz, 103–128 Hz); subband ratios; spectral edge frequency and spectral edge power at 50%.
  - *Time Domain*: Four statistical moments (mean, variance, skewness, kurtosis), Hjörth parameters (activity, mobility, complexity), and decorrelation time.
  - *Time-Frequency Domain*: Wavelet detail coefficient energies (D1 to D5 using Daubechies 4 wavelet).
- **Feature Selection & Optimization**:
  - Evaluated $k \in \{3, 5, 7, 10, 15, 20, 30\}$ features.
  - Logistic Regression: ANOVA $F$-test.
  - SVM Ensemble: Random Forest feature importance ranking.
  - SNN Ensemble: All 1,121 features retained.

### Classification Methodology
- **Classification Paradigm**: Strict `patient-specific` (models trained on first 3 seizures of a patient, tested on subsequent seizures of the same patient).
- **Models Evaluated**:
  1. *Weighted Logistic Regression*.
  2. *15 SVM Ensemble*: Linear kernel, cost hyperparameter $C \in \{2^{-10}, 2^{-8}, \dots, 2^8\}$ optimized via LOOCV. Majority voting across 15 models.
  3. *15 SNN Ensemble*: Input layer (1121), Dropout (0.5), FC layer (2 units), Softmax output (1 unit). Trained with Adam ($\text{lr} = 3 \times 10^{-4}$), binary cross-entropy, 500 epochs, early stopping patience 50. Majority voting across 15 models.
- **Post-Processing & Output Logic (Firing Power)**:
  - Calculated moving average of classifier outputs: $fp[n] = \frac{1}{\tau} \sum_{k=n-\tau}^n O[k]$.
  - *Prediction Branch*: Discrete alarm triggered when $fp[n] \ge 0.7$, followed by a refractory lockout equal to $\text{SPH} + \text{SOP}$.
  - *Forecasting Branch*: Continuous tri-level risk stratification: Low risk ($fp[n] \le 0.3$), Moderate risk ($0.3 < fp[n] \le 0.7$), High risk ($fp[n] > 0.7$).
- **Surrogate Statistical Validation**:
  - 30 surrogate time-series simulations per patient randomly placing seizure times in interictal recordings. Improvement over Chance (IoC) measures the percentage of patients where actual performance significantly exceeded surrogate levels ($p < 0.05$).

### Methodological Validity & Leakage Audit
- **Patient-Disjoint Status**: Not applicable (explicitly designed as a patient-specific evaluation).
- **Balancing Timing**: Applied strictly inside training data (safe).
- **Tuning Isolation**: Hyperparameters (SOP duration, $k$ features, SVM $C$) were tuned exclusively using 3-fold cross-validation on the 3 training seizures (optimizing geometric mean of sensitivity and specificity). The testing set (remaining seizures) was strictly evaluated out-of-sample.

# Conclusions

### Quantitative Metrics & Performance
- **Primary Results (Table 1, Event-Level Metrics across 40 Patients)**:
  - **Logistic Regression**:
    - *Prediction*: Seizure Sensitivity (SS) = **0.13 ± 0.26**, FPR/h = **0.36 ± 0.40**, IoC SS = **12.5%** (5 of 40 patients).
    - *Forecasting*: Seizure Sensitivity (SS) = **0.28 ± 0.37** (**+115% relative increase**), Time in Warning (TiW) = **0.13 ± 0.14**, Brier Score (BS) = **0.19 ± 0.11**, Brier Skill Score (BSS) = **0.01 ± 0.15**, IoC SS = **37.5%** (15 of 40), IoC BS = **50%** (20 of 40, **+300% increase**).
  - **15 SVM Ensemble**:
    - *Prediction*: SS = **0.13 ± 0.21**, FPR/h = **0.73 ± 0.85**, IoC SS = **17.5%**.
    - *Forecasting*: SS = **0.32 ± 0.37** (**+146% increase**), TiW = **0.19 ± 0.17**, BS = **0.23 ± 0.13**, BSS = **-0.01 ± 0.14**, IoC SS = **32.5%**, IoC BS = **47.5%**.
  - **15 SNN Ensemble**:
    - *Prediction*: SS = **0.12 ± 0.20**, FPR/h = **0.48 ± 0.66**, IoC SS = **20%**.
    - *Forecasting*: SS = **0.22 ± 0.33** (**+83% increase**), TiW = **0.13 ± 0.14**, BS = **0.18 ± 0.11**, BSS = **-0.04 ± 0.18**, IoC SS = **25%**, IoC BS = **45%**.
- **Computational Overhead**:
  - Logistic Regression required on average **7.34 seconds per patient**, whereas the 15 SVM ensemble required **196.18 seconds per patient**.
- **Related-Work Benchmark (Tables 2 & 3)**:
  - Contrasted with EPILEPSIAE prediction studies: Lopes et al. 2023 (SS 0.34, FPR/h 0.90, IoC 51%), Pinto et al. 2022 (SS 0.16, FPR/h 0.21, IoC 32%), Alvarado-Rojas et al. 2014 (SS 0.47, FPR/h 0.94, IoC 13%).
  - Contrasted with ultra-long-term forecasting studies (NeuroVista / sub-scalp): Cook et al. 2013 (SS 0.61, TiW 0.23, IoC 90%), Karoly et al. 2017 (SS 0.55, TiW 0.25, BSS 0.05, IoC 100%), Viana et al. 2022 (SS 0.74, TiW 0.31, IoC 83%).

### Key Takeaways & Contributions
- Proves mathematically and phenomenologically why forecasting achieves up to 146% higher sensitivity than prediction:
  1. *Elimination of Refractory Lockout*: Early preictal alarms do not lock the model out; the model stays in high risk during the SOP.
  2. *Accommodation of Brain Self-Termination*: Physiologically aborted seizures are appropriately treated as temporary risk elevations rather than penalized as false alarms.
- Demonstrates that weighted Logistic Regression outperforms complex neural and SVM ensembles in speed (26x faster) and calibration (only model with positive Brier Skill Score).

### Limitations & Gaps
- **(a) Author-Stated Limitations**:
  - The EPILEPSIAE database is a short presurgical dataset (a few days per patient) biased by medication withdrawal and sleep deprivation, altering baseline brain function and artificially clustering seizures.
  - Uniform fixed risk thresholds (0.3 and 0.7) were imposed across all patients rather than self-calibrating thresholds.
  - Did not consider circadian rhythms or sleep-wake cycle concept drifts.
- **(b) Methodological Limitations [Summarizer's Note]**:
  - Low Absolute Seizure Sensitivity: Even with forecasting, mean sensitivity is only 0.28–0.32, meaning over two-thirds of seizures were not forecast in the high-risk state.
  - Negative Skill Scores in Ensembles: Both SVM and SNN ensembles obtained negative Brier Skill Scores (-0.01 and -0.04), indicating they performed worse than an uninformed reference forecast.
  - Supplementary Tables S1–S4 were not accessible in the available text.
- **Future Work**:
  - Online continuous model retraining with each new seizure, testing on ultra-long-term ambulatory sub-scalp databases (months/years), and integrating circadian phase modeling.
