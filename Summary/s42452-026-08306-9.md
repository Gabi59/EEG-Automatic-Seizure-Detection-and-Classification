  - Fixed 23 bipolar scalp channels resampled to 256 Hz.

# Data sumary

### Data Preprocessing & Segmentation
- **Segment Structure**:
  - Window length: 1.0 second (256 samples per channel).
  - Overlap: Non-overlapping (0% overlap). Chosen to eliminate adjacent-window redundancy and avoid optimistic leakage.
- **Artifact Handling**:
  - `Baseline drift correction, channel normalization, and bandpass filtering (0.5–45 Hz)`. Uninformative low-variance features were excluded. No dedicated neural or ICA artifact-removal module was applied.
- **Signal Quality & Anomaly Handling**:
  - Dimensionality verified ($23 \times 256$); malformed segments discarded.

### Feature Extraction & Dimensionality Reduction
- **Multi-Domain Candidate Feature Extraction**:
  1. *Time Domain (per channel)*: Mean, variance, skewness, kurtosis, Root Mean Square (RMS), Line Length (LL), Zero-Crossing Rate (ZCR).
  2. *Frequency Domain*: Welch power spectral density with Hann window across Delta (0.5–4 Hz), Theta (4–8 Hz), Alpha (8–13 Hz), Beta (13–30 Hz), Gamma (30–45 Hz), and Spectral Edge Frequency (SEF95).
  3. *Wavelet Domain*: Discrete Wavelet Transform (db4, level 4: subband log-variance, relative energy, standard deviation) and Wavelet Packet Decomposition (db4, level 3 packet energies and variances).
  4. *Spatial Filtering (CSP)*: Regularized Common Spatial Patterns with shrinkage parameter $\lambda = 0.1$. Log-variance of projections and Beta/Gamma band power on CSP components.
- **Two-Step Feature Selection (Train Folds Only)**:
  - Step 1: Mutual Information (MI) ranking selecting top 50 features.
  - Step 2: Sequential Forward Selection (SFS) with 5-fold inner cross-validation selecting the optimal subset of **15 features** (performance plateaus at 13–15 features).
  - Selected features combine spatial (csp3logvar), temporal (ch2/ch21 variance, ch3/ch21 RMS), frequency (ch1/ch3 theta power, ch14/ch20 alpha power), and wavelet (ch1/ch2/ch20 DWT and WPD energies).

### Classification Methodology
- **Classification Paradigm**: `patient-independent` (70/30 patient-stratified split with nested 5-fold CV).
- **Classifiers Evaluated**:
  1. *Random Forest (RF)*: $n_{\text{estimators}} = 100$, $\text{max\_depth} = 15$, $\text{min\_samples\_split} = 2$, $\text{min\_samples\_leaf} = 1$.
  2. *K-Nearest Neighbours (KNN)*: $k = 7$, distance weighting, Manhattan metric.
  3. *Support Vector Machine (SVM)*: RBF kernel, $C = 1.0$, $\gamma = \text{'scale'}$, probability calibration with Standard Scaler.
- **Class Balancing Strategy**: Not reported. (Confusion matrices show roughly balanced predictions, but whether SMOTE, undersampling, or weights were applied is not specified).
- **Post-Processing & Decision Logic**: Direct window-level prediction without temporal smoothing or refractory periods.

### Methodological Validity & Leakage Audit
- **Patient-Disjoint Status**: Yes. Text states: *"The data were split such that different patients only appeared either in the training, validation, or test sets... complete patient exclusivity across the training, validation, and test sets."*
- **Balancing Timing**: Not stated.
- **Tuning Isolation**: Yes. Spatial filter learning (CSP covariance estimation), Mutual Information ranking, SFS, and classifier grid search were performed exclusively within training folds using 5-fold inner cross-validation. The test set was never exposed during feature selection or hyperparameter tuning.

# Conclusions

### Quantitative Metrics & Performance
- **Window-Level Test Performance (Table 1 & Section 4.5)**:
  - **Random Forest (Best Overall)**:
    - Accuracy: **90.5% ± 1.2%**
    - AUC: **0.927 ± 0.015**
    - Precision: **91%**
    - Recall (Sensitivity): **90%**
  - **K-Nearest Neighbours ($k=7$)**:
    - Accuracy: **90.6% ± 1.4%**
    - AUC: **0.904 ± 0.018**
    - Sensitivity: **89%**, Specificity: **92%**
  - **Support Vector Machine (RBF)**:
    - Accuracy: **88.1% ± 1.6%**
    - AUC: **0.881 ± 0.021**
- **Statistical Significance**:
  - Paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test (Bonferroni-corrected $\alpha = 0.0167$): Random Forest significantly outperformed SVM ($p = 0.012$, effect size $r = 0.68$). RF vs. KNN difference was not significant ($p = 0.089$).
  - Welch's $t$-test with Benjamini-Hochberg FDR correction: 14 of the 15 selected features showed statistically significant differences between seizure and non-seizure windows at $p < 0.001$.
- **Related-Work Comparison (Table 1)**:
  - Benchmarked against Ingolfsson et al. 2024 (Sensitivity 65.27% on CHB-MIT), Acharya et al. 2012 (>97% on curated Bonn segments), and Mekruksavanich et al. 2024 hybrid CNN (>99% on mixed CV).
- **Computational Overhead**:
  - End-to-end processing of a 1-second window completes in **under 50 milliseconds**.
  - Sustained memory footprint: **< 100 MB**.

### Key Takeaways & Contributions
- Proves that a compact feature space of just 15 multi-domain features (combining CSP spatial filters, wavelets, and PSD) allows traditional classifiers (Random Forest, KNN) to achieve >90% accuracy and >0.92 AUC without complex deep networks.
- Extremely lightweight inference (<50 ms per window) makes the pipeline suitable for near real-time edge monitoring.

### Limitations & Gaps
- **(a) Author-Stated Limitations**:
  - 1-second non-overlapping windows cannot capture slow transitional dynamics at seizure onset and offset.
  - Limited to the pediatric CHB-MIT dataset with a fixed 23-channel montage; has not been tested on multi-center or adult cohorts.
  - Channel-specific features (e.g., channels 2 and 20) were selected, which may limit generalizability to montages with different channel orders.
- **(b) Methodological Limitations [Summarizer's Note]**:
  - Not Strict LOPO: Evaluated on a single 70/30 patient split rather than full leave-one-patient-out cross-validation across all subjects.
  - Dataset Details Missing: Total recording hours, total seizure counts, and class balancing strategies are not reported.
  - Lack of FAR/h Accounting: Reports window-level accuracy, precision, and recall, but does not provide false alarms per hour (FAR/h) or detection latency over continuous recordings.
- **Future Work**:
  - Developing channel-agnostic spatial representations, channel reduction for wearable devices, adaptive patient calibration layers, and evaluating multi-center datasets.
