# General sumary

### Paper Identification & Metadata
- **Title**: Patient-independent EEG seizure detection using channel attention and dual-branch time–frequency features
- **Authors**: Hrishikesh Tiwary, Arnav Bhavsar, and Muralikrishna H.
- **Affiliation**: Indian Institute of Technology Mandi (IIT Mandi) and Manipal Institute of Technology (MAHE), India
- **Publication Venue**: *Neural Computing and Applications* (2026), 38:791, https://doi.org/10.1007/s00521-026-12518-w
- **Paper Type**: `proposal`
- **Task**: `detection` (patient-independent binary seizure vs. non-seizure detection with segment-to-episode aggregation and continuous-stream evaluation)

### Population & Clinical Setting
- **Subject Population**: Combined paediatric (CHB-MIT cohort: ages 1.5 to 22 years) and mixed adult/paediatric clinical patients (TUSZ v2.0.3 corpus).
- **Clinical Environment**: Inpatient clinical epilepsy monitoring units and multi-year hospital EEG recordings.

### Dataset & Volume Overview
- **Datasets**:
  1. *CHB-MIT Scalp EEG Database*: 24 pediatric cases recorded at native 256 Hz.
     - *Subject Inconsistencies*: Case chb24 has age and sex unrecorded; chb21 is known to be a subsequent recording from the chb01 individual.
     - *Montage*: Standardized across 18 common bipolar channels.
  2. *TUH Seizure Corpus (TUSZ v2.0.3)*: Large-scale clinical corpus resampled to 250 Hz.
     - *Volume & Official Split*:
       - Train: 579 subjects, 4,664 recordings, 910.3 hours, 2,138 seizure / 3,792 non-seizure events.
       - Dev: 53 subjects, 1,832 recordings, 435.5 hours, 978 seizure / 1,507 non-seizure events.
       - Eval: 43 subjects, 865 recordings, 127.7 hours, 469 seizure / 670 non-seizure events.
       - Total: 675 subjects, 7,361 recordings, 1,473.5 hours, 3,585 seizure / 5,969 non-seizure events.
     - *Montage*: Standardized to 20 temporal-central-parasagittal bipolar channels.
- **Exclusions**: Non-common bipolar derivations were discarded so all subjects within each dataset share a uniform channel set (18 channels for CHB-MIT, 20 channels for TUSZ).

# Data sumary

### Data Preprocessing & Segmentation
- **Segment Structure**:
  - Window length: $\Delta = 1.0\text{ s}$ with 50% overlap (hop = 0.5 s).
  - Label Assignment: Continuous expert-annotated seizure intervals and background intervals are defined as "episodes". Each episode is partitioned into 1s windows; every window inherits the label of its parent episode.
  - Window sensitivity analysis confirmed 1s windows with 50% overlap outperform 0.5s, 2s, 4s, and 0%, 25%, 75% overlap.
- **Artifact Handling**:
  - `None applied (the study does not employ an artifact-gating or artifact-removal module)`. Robustness to artifacts is handled through adaptive front-end channel attention and dual-branch feature representations.
- **Filtering & Frequency Harmonisation**:
  - Native 256 Hz for CHB-MIT; TUSZ resampled from 250–1000 Hz to 250 Hz. Signals processed through DWT multi-resolution dyadic subbands and data-adaptive EMD sifting.

### Feature Extraction & Dimensionality Reduction
- **Input Channel-Wise Attention**:
  - Operates on raw 1s window $X \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times N}$. Global average pooling over time produces channel summary $s \in \mathbb{R}^C$.
  - Bottleneck MLP with ReLU and Sigmoid produces channel weights $g \in \mathbb{R}^C$, reweighting channels prior to decomposition: $\tilde{X}_{c,t} = g_c \cdot X_{c,t}$.
- **Dual-Branch Time-Frequency Feature Construction**:
  - *Branch 1 (EMD)*: Adaptive Empirical Mode Decomposition extracts up to 7 Intrinsic Mode Functions (IMFs). Six statistical descriptors per IMF (mean, std, skewness, kurtosis, energy, Shannon entropy) yield a feature map $F^{(\text{EMD})} \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times 42}$.
  - *Branch 2 (DWT)*: 6-level discrete wavelet transform (db4 mother wavelet). Six statistical descriptors per subband yield a feature map $F^{(\text{DWT})} \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times 36}$.
- **Attention-Augmented CNN Backbones**:
  - Each branch is modeled by a shallow CNN (two Conv-BN-ReLU blocks: 32 and 64 filters, $3 \times 3$ kernels), followed by feature-wise attention, spatial attention, and Global Weighted Average Pooling (GWAP).
  - Pooled branch embeddings $e_{\text{EMD}}$ and $e_{\text{DWT}}$ are concatenated into $e_{\text{fused}}$.

### Classification Methodology
- **Classification Paradigm**: Strict `patient-independent` (Leave-One-Subject-Out cross-validation for CHB-MIT; official subject-disjoint train/dev/eval partition for TUSZ).
- **Architecture & Optimization**: Fully connected classification layer with dropout 0.5 and sigmoid activation. Trained using Adam optimizer ($\text{lr} = 10^{-3}$, batch size 32) up to 50 epochs with early stopping on validation F1-score.
- **Class Balancing Strategy**: Class-weighted binary cross-entropy loss combined with balanced mini-batch sampling (windows sampled such that each batch contains 50% seizure and 50% non-seizure windows).
- **Post-Processing & Episode-Level Aggregation**:
  - *Episode-Level Decision*: Window-level predicted probabilities $p_\ell$ within an episode are aggregated via soft voting: $s_j = \frac{1}{n_j} \sum_{\ell=1}^{n_j} p_\ell$, thresholded at 0.5.
  - *Continuous Recording Mode*: Test recordings processed sequentially without boundary knowledge. Consecutive positive windows merged into single alarm events; false alarms per hour (FAR/h) and detection latency measured.

### Methodological Validity & Leakage Audit
- **Patient-Disjoint Status**: Yes. In CHB-MIT LOSO, each held-out subject is completely excluded from training and validation. In TUSZ, official train (579 subjects), dev (53 subjects), and eval (43 subjects) partitions contain strictly non-overlapping patients.
- **Balancing Timing**: Applied strictly during training via online balanced mini-batch sampling.
- **Hyperparameter Tuning Isolation**: Hyperparameters and thresholding calibrated on validation subjects only; test sets strictly untouched.

# Conclusions

### Quantitative Metrics & Performance
- **Episode-Level Results (Full Proposed Model A8)**:
  - *CHB-MIT (LOSO, 24 Folds)*:
    - Accuracy: **91.63% ± 9.29%** (Table 4)
    - AUROC: **0.93 ± 0.07**
    - F1-Score: **90.25% ± 10.51%**
    - Recall (Sensitivity): **91.34% ± 10.13%**
  - *TUSZ v2.0.3 (Official Eval Split, 43 Subjects)*:
    - Accuracy: **92.87% ± 4.85%** (Table 5)
    - AUROC: **0.93 ± 0.05**
    - F1-Score: **92.65% ± 3.94%**
    - Recall (Sensitivity): **92.58% ± 4.66%**
- **Continuous-Recording Metrics (Table 8)**:
  - CHB-MIT (LOSO): False Alarm Rate = **0.30 ± 0.07 FAR/h**, Detection Latency = **3.22 ± 1.08 seconds**.
  - TUSZ (Eval): False Alarm Rate = **0.39 ± 0.12 FAR/h**, Detection Latency = **4.51 ± 1.32 seconds**.
- **Related-Work Comparison (Tables 13 & 14)**:
  - Outperformed recent subject-independent CHB-MIT models: Li et al. 2024 MGCNA (Acc 89.45%, F1 87.67%, Recall 84.72%), Liu et al. 2025 S-Transform CNN (Acc 92.10%, Recall 79.59%), Zhang et al. 2024 adversarial learning (Recall 88.04%).
  - Outperformed TUSZ benchmarks: Gui et al. 2024 VQ-MTM (AUROC 0.904), Huang et al. 2025 AFTA Transformer (AUROC 0.891).
- **Computational Latency & Runtime (Table 12)**:
  - EMD feature extraction: 115.95 ms; DWT extraction: 0.35 ms; CNN inference: 0.41 ms.
  - Total processing time per 1 s window: **116.71 ms** (well below the 500 ms window shift interval).

### Key Takeaways & Contributions
- Proves that combining data-adaptive non-stationary features (EMD) with multi-resolution structured features (DWT) outperforms either transform alone by 7%–12% accuracy.
- Introduces front-end channel-wise attention that reweights electrode importance dynamically, addressing spatial variability across patients prior to non-linear decomposition.

### Limitations & Gaps
- **(a) Author-Stated Limitations**:
  - No external multicenter hospital cohort outside CHB-MIT and TUSZ was evaluated.
  - Binary classification only; does not discriminate specific seizure types.
  - EMD sifting dominates 99% of computation time (115.95 ms out of 116.71 ms).
  - Susceptible to false alarms from non-ictal transients (muscle twitches, electrode pop) and misses very short (< 10s) or spatially focal seizures.
- **(b) Methodological Limitations [Summarizer's Note]**:
  - Metric Level Context: Episode-level accuracy (91.63%) aggregates multiple 1s windows through soft voting, which inherently smooths out window-level classification errors and produces higher apparent accuracy than raw window-level metrics.
  - Sifting Iterations: Fixed 7-IMF truncation merges higher-order residual modes into the 7th slot, potentially introducing signal-dependent boundary artifacts.
- **Future Work**:
  - Developing parallel/accelerated EMD algorithms, multi-class seizure type classification, and end-to-end sequence modeling for temporal onset/offset boundary refinement.
